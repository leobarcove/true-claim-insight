How a claim moves through the platform

Eleven flows covering a claimant's first notification through to the fee note — the intake funnel, the regulated engagement, and the clocks running underneath both. Every state machine here is transcribed from the running code, so a diagram and the server disagreeing is a defect in one of them, not a matter of interpretation.

"Medical" here is a travel claim's subtype, not a line of business. It means a claimant who fell ill or was injured on a trip, claiming under travel cover — TravelClaimType.MEDICAL. Standalone Medical & Health insurance is out of scope entirely (§1 of the plan excludes it alongside motor and Individual PA), so it never reaches this diagram at all. The subtype is refused a desk review before the economic tests, so a small medical claim cannot slip through on value: whether a condition was pre-existing, and whether treatment was necessary, are clinical judgements no rule set should make.

The firm recommends; the insurer decides. Nothing in this system settles a claim. Step 7 is a handback, and the adjuster report says so on its face.

Step 4 decides how the claim is examined. Four conditions must all hold for desk review, and the mode is disclosed in the report as a PD 12.6 method — "desk review on documents alone" and "site inspection" reach the same figure by different means.

03

A claimant submits a case

Conversational intake: one question at a time, resumable through currentStepId, with the question set chosen by travel claim type.

One engine, two channels, as of 11 August 2026. The PWA used to walk the flow itself — the page chose the next step and patched each answer — so every rule existed twice and only the messaging copy was maintained. It now posts turns to the same ConversationGateway that answers Telegram and renders the transcript it gets back. What the browser gains for free is everything built on the messaging side and never ported: the transcript itself, back and edit, the progress count, and a route to a human that web claimants did not previously have at all.

Two flags are computed at submission, both advisory. notifiedLate marks notification more than 24 hours after the incident; outOfWindow marks more than 30 days. They surface as warnings to the operator and never auto-reject — the deadline is the insurer's to enforce, not the intake form's.

The CSP evidence anchor lives on the Claim, not the Case. documentsCompleteAt — the fact the fourteen-working-day final-report window runs from (CSP 10.13) — is stamped when conversion copies a complete mandatory set across, or on the later upload that completes it. Set once and never moved: the documents were complete, and the clock that started from that fact stays honest.

The loop INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW may run any number of times. Every pass writes an audit row carrying before and after values.

Two things the panel says about identity. The claimant's name is shown as "Stated at intake · not verified" until the case converts — the name is what the claimant typed, no eKYC vendor is integrated, and a panel that displayed it plainly would imply a check nobody performed.

The payee name check compares that name against the account holder. A divergence is usually innocent — a parent, a spouse, a company card — and is also what payout diversion looks like, so it warns and never blocks. The verdict is three-valued: only a match silences the warning, and uncertain exists so borderline Malaysian names are not forced into a false accusation. Banks drop bin, binti and a/p, truncate the account-name field around twenty characters, and Chinese name order is arbitrary — so Wong Chee Keong against Wong Chi Kiong is referred to the operator rather than guessed at. It screens nobody: AMLA/CTF screening remains Gate G8.

The report cites a worksheet revision. Creating a report pre-fills the PD 12.6 quantum section from the current worksheet and records which revision it used, so the figure in the report and the workings behind it cannot silently diverge. A draft citing a superseded revision reports itself as outdated; an issued report keeps the figure it was signed against.

Segregation of duties. The assessor cannot decide their own claim unless an AuthorityLimit expressly permits it, and never above its monetary ceiling. The basis is written onto the audit row, so the question "on whose authority?" has an answer years later.

CSP 10.13 has two limbs, and the second one matters most on the days that matter most. The clause sets the fourteen-working-day non-motor window and excuses it in exceptional circumstances — complex claims, catastrophe and large-event losses, suspected fraud. Without machinery for the second, a Klang Valley flood would have shown as mass firm failure: every clock breaching, every claim escalating, and nothing on file to say the policy document permitted the delay. That is wrong in the direction that costs a firm its panel.

The relief is deliberately hard to claim. A named ground from the PD's own list, a written reason, a bounded number of working days someone chose, an attributable actor and an audit row — never automatic, and never twice on one clock, because buying time in small increments is how a deadline quietly stops meaning anything. A clock already breached against the old window returns to running: that breach measured a deadline the PD did not require.

The escalation now reaches a person. A firm-owned breach emails the operations address, deduplicated per clock and per level so a breach left open over a weekend sends once rather than every fifteen minutes. Insurer-side stages are measured and never escalated against the firm, so they send nothing.

The answer goes through CasesService.patchAnswer rather than a second write path. That is what keeps the masking of the bank account number, the policy match and the deadline warnings — and it is why a Telegram sender provably cannot reach another claimant's case: the same assertAccess check runs as in the browser. Attribution differs by author: a claimant's turns leave the conversation transcript, which is their record; a staff correction has no transcript, so it goes through a separate, audited endpoint that records actor and before/after values and never moves the conversational cursor (MASTER_PLAN §6 item 21).

Binding is the platform's own verified contact — Share my number — and a typed number is refused, because a contact card from an address book carries somebody else's. The adapter accepts it only when Telegram says the contact belongs to the sender. No one-time code is sent on this channel (decided 11 Aug 2026): the code would go to the very number the platform had already vouched for. The code survives where it earns its keep — the public web-chat door, where a browser can claim any number; five wrong codes there burn the pending number and the claimant starts that step again.

Every message is stored in both directions, and an outbound one records whether the bot or a named agent sent it. That single distinction is what makes bot performance reviewable — and it is why the operator inbox can show three voices rather than two. When an agent takes over, the bot stands down entirely until the conversation is handed back.

9b·2

WhatsApp intake

The same conversation as §9b, on the channel most Malaysians already use. Identical flow, identical questions, identical rules — one engine, a different adapter.

Nothing about the conversation is duplicated. The gateway decides the next question, validates the answer and advances the Case against the flow pinned on it; the adapter knows only how to say things on WhatsApp. Everything built for the messaging side — back and edit, the progress count, the answer summary at review, handover to a person — arrives here for free because there is one implementation of it.

Three platform differences that are not cosmetic. Identity arrives free — wa_id is on every inbound message, so there is no "Share my number" step and nothing to spoof. Choices render as an interactive list capped at ten rows, where Telegram holds about a hundred, so pagination genuinely fires here and renderChoices reserves a row for "More options". And a 24-hour service window applies: replies inside it are free-form and free of charge, but a conversation left overnight cannot be reopened by us without an approved template. That is not a dead end — it is why one exists. tci_claim_information_request (UTILITY) carries the returned-case ask to a claimant who has gone quiet, which is precisely the claimant such an ask is for. It is spent only when the ordinary free-form push is refused, never when that lands, and a template that arrives is written into the transcript like any other outbound word — it is still the firm's word to the claimant. The claimant's reply reopens the window, and the ordinary conversation resumes at no cost. See docs/WHATSAPP_TEMPLATES.md; with no template configured the channel simply waits for their next message.

Push, not poll. Meta delivers to a webhook, so there is no offset to advance and no singleton constraint — every instance may receive updates, and the gateway's insert-first dedupe on the message id is what makes that safe. The Telegram footgun, where two pollers each take half the updates and claimants appear to be intermittently ignored, does not exist on this channel.

The endpoint is public, because Meta is unauthenticated to us, so the X-Hub-Signature-256 HMAC is the only thing between it and anyone who can POST JSON — and the payload names a claimant's phone number. With no app secret configured every delivery is discarded rather than trusted. It always answers 200: Meta retries a non-200 for seven days, and a payload that can never succeed would be redelivered all week with every later message queued behind it.

Offshore, like Telegram. Message content reaches Meta in the United States and is retained in WhatsApp's own history, beyond our retention sweep. Every turn writes a TransferRecord with lawfulBasis: null, because none is established — synthetic and internal-tester data only until it is (MASTER_PLAN §3.4).

How a flow is authored

One structure, many channels and languages. What can differ per channel is wording — and only wording, by the shape of the data rather than by a rule.

An overlay has no next and no answerType. A channel therefore cannot diverge into asking different questions — the guarantee is in the type, not in a convention a later import tool could bypass. A channel that genuinely needs a shorter conversation gets its own definition and is pointed at it; it does not get a clone that drifts.

Locale outranks channel when both overlay the same step. Wrong tone is cosmetic; wrong language is a comprehension failure, and PDPA s.7 treats the language of a claimant-facing notice as substantive.

Overlays are deliberately unversioned. Because they carry only copy, they cannot move the conversational cursor or invalidate a stored answer — so a typo can be corrected live, with conversations in flight. Only structure needs pinning, which is why the pin sits on the definition and the Case.

The rules decide; a model only reads. Nothing here is an LLM choosing what to ask. When a claimant's answer defeats deterministic parsing, a model may be asked to interpret that one message into a value — and that value passes the same validation as a typed one before it goes anywhere. The model never sees the flow, never picks the next question, never writes to a claim. It is off by default, and bounded that way so every question asked stays traceable to a published flow version rather than to a model's judgement on the day.

The operator inbox

Where the firm watches every channel's conversations, and steps in when the bot cannot continue. Three panes: the queue, the transcript, and the claim beside it.

Taking over pauses the intake — the screen says so. A generic support console treats a slow reply as a service problem; here it is a claim that has stopped being filled in while a CSP clock runs. The banner above the transcript states it, the queue's wait chip escalates from amber to red as the wait ages, and the sort puts the longest-owed conversation first rather than the newest.

The claim travels with the message. The context panel keeps the case, its intake progress and every document the conversation has collected beside the thread — an agent who must open another tab to see what was being claimed answers the wrong question. The same panel carries the channel's retention warning in full: on Telegram or WhatsApp, what an agent types persists on the platform's servers, and the moment that is worth saying is before they type it.

Three voices stay visibly distinct — claimant, bot, agent — and an internal note is a fourth thing entirely: amber, dashed, centred, never sent. The take-over moment is marked inline in the transcript where the change of author happens, because that is exactly where an auditor will ask who was speaking and why.

Small-claims fast-track

Four conditions must all hold before a claim takes the desk-review route, and any one of three triggers pulls it back out mid-flight.

The thresholds are the firm's, set per tenant. Categories and per-category ceilings live in firm configuration, alongside licensed mode and the working-day calendar — so a white-labelled insurer's claims are routed on that insurer's rules rather than a platform default.

Configuration gaps fail closed. A category with no limit configured is not fast-tracked, and an unknown estimated amount is not treated as a small one — both refuse the fast track rather than passing a test against nothing. Medical is excluded ahead of the economic checks, so a small medical claim cannot slip through on value.
